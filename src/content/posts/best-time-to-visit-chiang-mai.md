Chiang Mai works well for remote workers for most of the year, but not all of it. The cool season from November to mid-February is the clear favourite, the rainy season offers the best value, and the burning season in March and April is the one period many nomads plan around. Here is how the year breaks down.

## When is the best time to visit Chiang Mai as a digital nomad?

**November to mid-February** is the best window. Days are dry and warm, mornings and evenings are cool enough for a light jacket, and the air is usually clean. It is also the busiest time of year, so book accommodation early and expect the popular work cafés to fill up by mid-morning.

If cost and quiet matter more than sunshine, **June to October** is the strongest alternative. Avoid committing to **March and April** unless you have checked current air-quality readings and are prepared to spend most of the day indoors.

## What are Chiang Mai's seasons?

Chiang Mai has three broad seasons, plus a fourth that locals and long-stayers talk about just as much.

- **Cool and dry (November to February):** daytime highs in the mid to high 20s °C, with January nights dropping to around 15°C. Almost no rain from December to February.
- **Hot (March to May):** temperatures climb steadily, peaking in April with average highs around 36°C.
- **Rainy (late May to October):** August and September are the wettest months, each averaging over 200mm of rain. The countryside turns green and temperatures ease a little.
- **Burning or smoke season (roughly late February to April):** overlaps with the hot season and is the main reason nomads leave.

## What is the burning season, and how bad is it?

Every year, agricultural burning and forest fires across northern Thailand and neighbouring countries fill the Chiang Mai valley with smoke. The surrounding mountains and still, dry-season air trap it, and fine particulate matter (PM2.5) can sit at unhealthy levels for weeks. NASA notes that fire activity typically peaks in March and fades by May as the rains return.

It should not be played down. In early April 2026, Chiang Mai recorded AQI readings above 200 on IQAir and ranked among the most polluted major cities in the world for several days running. Yet the same season also had an unusually clear spell in late March, helped by stricter provincial burning controls and thunderstorms. For 2026, the province announced a ban on open burning from 1 January to 31 May. Enforcement helps, but it has not ended the problem.

The practical advice:

- **If you have asthma, a heart or lung condition, or are travelling with children,** plan to be elsewhere from late February to April.
- **If you stay,** choose a condo with sealed windows and a proper air purifier, keep N95 masks to hand, and favour coworking spaces with filtered air over open-sided cafés.
- **Check live data,** using IQAir or the Thai government's Air4Thai app, rather than relying on how the sky looks.

Many long-term residents simply treat March and April as their travel months, heading to the south of Thailand or abroad.

## What is Chiang Mai like in the hot season?

Once the smoke clears in late April or May, the heat remains. April is the hottest month, and an air-conditioned condo stops being a luxury. Electricity bills climb, particularly in buildings that charge above the standard government rate, so ask about the per-unit price before signing. **Songkran**, the Thai New Year, falls on 13 to 15 April as a national holiday, and Chiang Mai celebrates with one of the country's biggest water fights around the Old City moat. It is great fun for a day or two, but expect closures and very little getting done that week.

## Is the rainy season a good time for digital nomads?

For many remote workers it is underrated. Rain typically arrives in heavy afternoon or evening bursts rather than all-day drizzle, so mornings are often workable and bright. The hills around Doi Suthep turn green, the air is clean, and the city is noticeably calmer.

The downsides are real, though. Scooter riding in a downpour is unpleasant, some roads flood briefly after storms, and in 2024 the Ping River burst its banks and flooded parts of the city, including areas near the river. If you plan a long stay from August to October, check flood history before choosing a building near the water. Choosing [the right area to live in](/best-areas-to-live-in-chiang-mai/) matters more in these months.

## How do crowds and costs change through the year?

Demand follows the weather. December to February is peak season, with the highest short-stay prices and the most competition for well-located condos in Nimman. The Yi Peng and Loy Krathong lantern festivals, expected on 24 to 25 November in 2026, bring a sharp spike in visitors and hotel rates around those dates.

The rainy season is the quietest period, and landlords and hotels are more open to negotiation. Whatever the month, lease length is the bigger lever: month-to-month rates are always the highest, and landlords commonly take 10 to 15% off for a 12-month commitment. For a full breakdown of rent, food and coworking, see [what a month in Chiang Mai really costs](/cost-of-living-chiang-mai-digital-nomads/).

- **Busiest and priciest:** December to February, plus Yi Peng in November.
- **Shoulder:** November, May.
- **Quietest and best value:** June to October, and March to April (because of the smoke).

## Which months suit a digital nomad best?

- **November:** excellent. Cool, clear and lively, with Yi Peng late in the month.
- **December to January:** the best weather of the year, but the busiest. Book ahead.
- **February:** good early on, with smoke often starting to build towards the end.
- **March to April:** the hardest months. Consider leaving or prepare carefully.
- **May:** hot, with the smoke gone and rains starting. Quiet and decent value.
- **June to October:** green, calm and affordable. Ideal for focused work if rain does not bother you.

## How should you plan a longer stay?

Arrive in October or November to secure a condo before peak demand, stay through the cool season, and plan a trip away for March and April. Return in May or June if you want to experience the green season. During the busier months, arrive early at popular [work cafés](/best-cafes-work-chiang-mai/) or take a coworking membership to guarantee a desk. If your stay spans several seasons, check the current visa rules on official Thai government sources before booking, as entry conditions change.
