For most remote workers, the best day trips from Chiang Mai are Doi Suthep and Doi Pui (half a day, close to town), Doi Inthanon (the classic mountain day) and the Sticky Waterfall (free and unusual). Most of the eight below sit within two hours of the city.

Timing matters. The cool season from November to February brings clear views, while smoke in March and April can hide them completely, as the guide to the [best time to visit Chiang Mai](/best-time-to-visit-chiang-mai/) explains. Waterfalls are fullest in the rainy season, when mountain roads are also most slippery.

## 1. Doi Suthep and Doi Pui — best for a half day

Wat Phra That Doi Suthep overlooks the city from the mountain west of Nimman.

- **Getting there:** about 15 km, 30–40 minutes by road; shared red songthaews run up from town, or take a Grab or scooter.
- **Cost:** temple entry ฿30 for foreigners; shared songthaew ฿50–80 per person each way; a private songthaew around ฿400–700 return with waiting time.
- **Who it suits:** anyone with a free morning, including those without their own transport.
- **Upsides:** close, easy and impressive, with a city view from the terrace.
- **Downsides:** crowded by mid-morning, and the view disappears in the burning season.

The temple is reached by a naga-lined staircase of around 300 steps, or a small funicular for a modest extra fee. Arrive at sunrise for the quietest visit. Fitter visitors can hike the Monk's Trail to Wat Pha Lat, a forest temple halfway up, and songthaews continue from the temple car park to the Hmong village at Doi Pui. Since October 2025, foreigners pay ฿100 for the national park's nature areas, including the trail and Doi Pui; the temples remain fee-free by road.

## 2. Doi Inthanon — best for mountain scenery

Thailand's highest mountain, at 2,565 metres, is the classic full-day trip.

- **Getting there:** about 100 km south-west, around 2 hours each way; tours and private drivers are widely available.
- **Cost:** national park entry ฿300 for foreign adults, plus a small vehicle fee; around ฿100 for the Royal Twin Pagodas.
- **Who it suits:** hikers, photographers and anyone wanting cool mountain air.
- **Upsides:** waterfalls, cloud forest, the twin pagodas and their gardens.
- **Downsides:** a long day, and a cold summit at dawn in the cool season.

The Kew Mae Pan nature trail, the highlight for many, is only open from November to May and charges its own guide fee. Bring a warm layer: summit temperatures in December and January can drop close to freezing.

## 3. Bua Thong Sticky Waterfall — best free day out

Bua Thong is a tiered waterfall whose limestone deposits give a grippy surface, so visitors can walk straight up the cascades.

- **Getting there:** about 60 km north, roughly 1 hour 20 minutes to 1.5 hours by car or scooter.
- **Cost:** free entry; open roughly 8am to 5pm.
- **Who it suits:** groups of friends and anyone after something hands-on.
- **Upsides:** unusual, free, with toilets and food stalls at the car park.
- **Downsides:** busy on weekends, and Grab drivers often decline the return leg, so arrange transport both ways.

Chet Si, the spring-fed pool at the top, is a pleasant place to cool off. Arrive by 9am to beat the crowds.

## 4. Mae Kampong — best for a cool escape

A hillside village of wooden houses, cafés and homestays strung along a stream east of the city.

- **Getting there:** about 50 km, 1–1.5 hours up a steep, winding road; songthaews reach Ban Mae On, but the final 14 km needs private transport or a homestay pick-up.
- **Cost:** no entry fee; budget for coffee and lunch.
- **Who it suits:** confident drivers, coffee lovers and anyone wanting a slower pace.
- **Upsides:** cool air, forest scenery, and the option to stay overnight.
- **Downsides:** the road suits experienced riders only, and parking is tight.

The village grows tea and coffee, and its streamside cafés make a calmer weekend alternative to the [work cafés of Nimman](/best-cafes-work-chiang-mai/).

## 5. An ethical elephant sanctuary — best for wildlife

Several sanctuaries in the surrounding hills let visitors observe and feed rescued elephants.

- **Getting there:** usually 1–1.5 hours by road, with hotel pick-up included.
- **Cost:** around ฿2,500–3,500 for a full day, typically including lunch.
- **Who it suits:** animal lovers and visiting friends and family.
- **Upsides:** a rare chance to watch elephants up close in a natural setting.
- **Downsides:** quality varies widely, and the best places book up in high season.

Choose only places that ban riding, performances and chains, keep groups small and let elephants roam. Many welfare groups also advise against bathing elephants, so check what is included.

## 6. Lamphun — best for history

Lamphun, once capital of the Hariphunchai kingdom that predates Chiang Mai, makes a quiet half-day trip south of the city.

- **Getting there:** about 30 km, around 40 minutes; blue songthaews leave from near [Warorot Market](/what-to-see-and-do-in-chiang-mai/), and the slow train costs under ฿20.
- **Cost:** Wat Phra That Hariphunchai entry is typically ฿20–50 for foreigners; the Hariphunchai National Museum opposite charges separately.
- **Who it suits:** temple and history enthusiasts, and those without their own transport.
- **Upsides:** cheap, uncrowded and easy by public transport.
- **Downsides:** little to do beyond the old town, and return train times are inconvenient.

The temple's golden chedi is one of the most revered in northern Thailand, and the ride back is simplest by songthaew.

## 7. Mon Cham — best for cool-season views

A hilltop royal project in Mae Rim, north of the city, with flower terraces, viewpoints and simple restaurants.

- **Getting there:** roughly an hour by car via the Mae Rim valley; the final climb is steep.
- **Cost:** entry is generally free; food and parking extra.
- **Who it suits:** couples and anyone with a car.
- **Upsides:** wide mountain views and fresh produce from the project farms.
- **Downsides:** hard to reach without your own wheels, and views are hazy in the burning season.

It is best from November to February, when mornings are crisp and the flowers bloom.

## 8. Chiang Rai's White Temple — best long day

Wat Rong Khun, the ornate white temple just south of Chiang Rai, is the furthest trip worth doing in a day.

- **Getting there:** roughly 3–3.5 hours each way by bus from Arcade Bus Terminal 3, then a short local ride.
- **Cost:** ฿200 entry for foreigners since January 2026; bus fares around ฿260–400 each way depending on class.
- **Who it suits:** those happy to trade six hours of travel for a striking sight.
- **Upsides:** unlike anything else in the region.
- **Downsides:** very little time on site unless you join a tour or stay the night.

It opens roughly 8am to 5.30pm, and many visitors book a day tour that adds the Blue Temple.

## Planning your trips

Weekdays and early starts make every trip easier. [Renting a scooter](/settling-in-chiang-mai/) or sharing a driver keeps costs low and fits easily within [the cost of living in Chiang Mai](/cost-of-living-chiang-mai-digital-nomads/). National park fees change from time to time, so confirm current prices before setting off.
