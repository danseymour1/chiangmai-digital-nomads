Chiang Mai might be the best café-working city on earth — there are more laptop-friendly spots here than you could visit in a year, most with fast wifi and coffee for a couple of pounds. Here is how to find your spot and settle in.

> This is a starter guide — swap in your own favourite cafés and first-hand notes, and check any details before relying on them.

## What makes a good work café here

Before the picks, the checklist that matters in Chiang Mai:

- **Fast wifi** — most modern cafés have solid fibre, but the busiest spots can slow at peak times.
- **Plug sockets** — the real bottleneck at popular cafés. Arrive early to claim one.
- **Space to stay** — larger cafés welcome long sessions; tiny specialty spots prefer you don't camp.
- **Air-con** — matters a lot in the hot months.

## Where the work cafés cluster

The densest cluster by far is **Nimmanhaemin ("Nimman")** — dozens of cafés and coworking spaces within walking distance, which is exactly why so many nomads base themselves nearby. The **Old City** and **Santitham** have their own quieter, often cheaper options.

## How to work café-style without annoying anyone

Thai café culture is relaxed, but a few habits keep you welcome:

- Buy a drink roughly every couple of hours you're there.
- Don't take a six-person table to yourself at lunchtime.
- Bring headphones and take calls quietly — or step outside.
- Tip the tip jar; it goes a long way.

## Always carry a backup

Even with good café wifi, keep a **mobile data SIM** topped up. If a café's connection wobbles mid-meeting, you can hotspot from your phone and carry on. It's the cheapest bit of insurance for remote work here.

## Prefer a desk?

If cafés aren't your style, Chiang Mai has one of the best selections of coworking spaces anywhere, with monthly memberships to suit every budget. Many nomads do both — a coworking base for focused days, cafés for a change of scene.
