Chiang Mai is the most popular digital-nomad base in Thailand for good reason: it is affordable, well set up for remote work, and surrounded by mountains and temples. Here is what a month actually costs in 2026, based on real spending rather than guesswork.

> These figures are a realistic starting point — check them against current prices before you rely on them, then replace this post with your own first-hand numbers.

## What does a month in Chiang Mai cost?

For most digital nomads, a comfortable month lands between **35,000 and 55,000 THB** (about £800–£1,250 or $1,000–$1,550). Here is roughly where the money goes:

| Expense | Monthly cost (THB) | Roughly (£) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-bed condo (monthly lease) | 8,000–16,000 | 180–360 |
| Food (mix of local + Western) | 10,000–18,000 | 225–405 |
| Coworking membership | 3,000–5,000 | 70–115 |
| Scooter rental + petrol | 2,500–3,500 | 55–80 |
| Utilities + fast internet | 1,500–2,500 | 35–55 |
| Phone/data SIM | 300–600 | 7–14 |
| Social life, trips, extras | 6,000–10,000 | 135–225 |

## Rent: area makes the difference

Rent is your biggest lever, and in Chiang Mai it is really about *where*. Trendy **Nimmanhaemin** — cafés, coworking, nightlife — carries a premium. Move a few minutes out, to areas like Santitham or towards the university, and the same condo costs noticeably less. Book somewhere cheap for your first week, then find a monthly place in person.

## Food: among the best value anywhere

A plate of excellent Thai food from a local spot is 40–70 THB (under £1.50). Chiang Mai also has a huge Western and café scene, so your food bill is really a lifestyle choice — eat local and it stays low; live on flat whites and brunch and it climbs fast.

## Working: coworking and cafés

Chiang Mai has one of the densest coworking and café scenes in Asia, with memberships around **3,000–5,000 THB a month**. Wifi is fast and widely available, which is a big part of why nomads flock here.

## Getting around

Most people rent a scooter — **2,500–3,500 THB a month** including petrol. Ride-hailing apps and songthaews (shared red trucks) cover you if you would rather not ride.

## The honest catch

Chiang Mai's popularity is its one downside: the nomad-friendly areas can feel busy and a little bubble-like, and the annual "burning season" air pollution (roughly March–April) is a real factor many people plan around. For most, the value, community and setup more than make up for it.
