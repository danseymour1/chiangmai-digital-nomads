For most remote workers who want to spend months rather than weeks in Chiang Mai, the answer is the **Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)**. It is valid for five years, gives up to 180 days per entry and can be extended once in Thailand. Shorter trips can still be made on visa exemption, but since 15 September 2026 that allows only 30 days for UK, US and most EU passports. The long-term alternatives (LTR, Thailand Privilege and education visas) suit narrower groups of people.

Thai visa rules change often, so before booking, check the current requirements on the official Thai e-Visa website (thaievisa.go.th) and with your nearest Thai embassy or consulate.

## How long can you stay in Thailand without a visa?

From 15 September 2026, nationals of 60 countries and territories, including the UK, the US, Canada, Australia and most EU states, can enter Thailand without a visa for up to **30 days**. This replaced the 60-day exemption introduced in July 2024.

- **Extension:** once, by 30 days, at an immigration office for ฿1,900, at the officer's discretion.
- **Land arrivals:** limited to two exemption entries a year.
- **Purpose:** the exemption is framed around tourism, not extended remote work.
- **Transition:** anyone stamped in on or before 14 September 2026 keeps the 60 days they were given.

All visitors also need to complete the online Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) before arriving.

## What is the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)?

The DTV launched in July 2024 for remote workers, freelancers and people taking part in "soft power" activities such as Muay Thai training or Thai cookery courses. Spouses and children of DTV holders can apply as dependants.

- **Validity:** five years, multiple entry.
- **Stay per entry:** up to 180 days.
- **Extension:** one extension of up to 180 days per entry, at an immigration office in Thailand for ฿1,900. Leaving and re-entering starts a fresh 180 days.
- **Financial proof:** at least ฿500,000, usually shown through recent bank statements. Embassies vary on how long the money must have been held.
- **Work evidence:** an employment contract or employer letter, company documents, or a freelance portfolio and invoices showing clients outside Thailand.
- **Fee:** ฿10,000 at missions that charge in baht; elsewhere, the local-currency equivalent.

Applications are made online through the Thai e-Visa website and assigned to the embassy or consulate covering the applicant's location, and cannot be made from inside Thailand. Approval can be quick, sometimes within a week, though times vary.

## What changed for the DTV in August 2026?

For applications submitted from 31 August 2026, the Thai Department of Consular Affairs replaced the old proof of current location with **proof of permanent residence**, and added a **criminal record certificate**. In practice, this means applying from your home country or the country where you hold residence, rather than during a short trip to a neighbouring country. Certificate requirements differ between embassies, so check your own embassy's checklist.

Immigration officers in Chiang Mai have reportedly asked some DTV holders for updated bank statements, proof of ongoing remote work and a rental contract when they apply for the 180-day extension. Extensions are discretionary, not automatic.

## Can you use a tourist visa instead?

The single-entry tourist visa (TR) gives a 60-day stay, extendable once by 30 days at an immigration office for ฿1,900, so around 90 days in total. It is applied for through the Thai e-Visa site before travelling, and the fee varies by embassy. With the exemption now cut to 30 days, the TR is a sensible option for a single stay of two to three months, but it is meant for tourism, not ongoing remote work.

## What is the LTR visa for remote workers?

The Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa, run by the Thailand Board of Investment, includes a **Work-from-Thailand Professional** category for people employed by large, established overseas companies. It is a 10-year visa (an initial five years, renewable for five more) costing ฿50,000 when issued in Thailand.

- **Income:** a high personal income threshold, set in US dollars and averaged over the previous two years, with a lower threshold for applicants holding a master's degree or above.
- **Employer:** a listed company, or an established private company meeting minimum trading history and revenue requirements.
- **Health cover:** qualifying health insurance, Thai social security, or a minimum bank deposit held for a year.
- **Perks:** an annual report replaces the 90-day report.

The criteria rule out most freelancers, but for senior employees of large firms it is the most stable option.

## Is Thailand Privilege worth it for digital nomads?

Thailand Privilege (formerly Thailand Elite) sells long-stay visa memberships rather than assessing income or work. Official membership fees range from **฿650,000 for Bronze** and **฿900,000 for Gold** (both five years) to ฿1,500,000 for Platinum (10 years), ฿2,500,000 for Diamond (15 years) and ฿5,000,000 for the invitation-only Reserve tier (20 years). It suits people who can afford the upfront cost, but grants no right to work in Thailand.

## Can you stay on an education visa?

The Non-Immigrant ED visa covers study at a recognised Thai institution, and in Chiang Mai that most often means a Thai language school. Schools and immigration now check attendance more closely than in the past, so it only makes sense for people who genuinely want to study. It is not a workaround for remote work.

## How do extensions and reporting work in Chiang Mai?

Extensions of stay are handled by Chiang Mai Immigration. In recent years, extensions have been processed at the office inside **Promenada Resort Mall** on the east side of the city, while the office's official website lists an address on Airport Road. Confirm which office handles your service before going, arrive early, and bring passport photos, passport copies and the ฿1,900 fee in cash.

- **90-day reporting (TM47):** required after 90 consecutive days in Thailand. It is free and can be filed online, by post or in person.
- **TM30:** your landlord or hotel must [notify immigration of your address](/settling-in-chiang-mai/), normally within 24 hours of arrival. Many condo owners in areas such as [Nimman and Santitham](/best-areas-to-live-in-chiang-mai/) handle this, but tenants should ask for a copy, as immigration often requests it at extension time.

## Does staying 180 days make you a Thai tax resident?

Anyone in Thailand for 180 days or more in a calendar year is generally treated as a Thai tax resident, whatever their visa. Since 2024, foreign income brought into Thailand by tax residents can be taxable. This is a general point only, not tax advice, so long-stayers should take advice from a qualified tax adviser. Budgeting for this alongside rent and living costs is part of [planning a longer stay in Chiang Mai](/cost-of-living-chiang-mai-digital-nomads/).

## Can you work for Thai clients on any of these visas?

No. None of the routes above allows work for Thai companies or Thai clients, paid or otherwise. They cover remote work for employers and customers outside Thailand only. Working locally requires a Thai work permit and the right non-immigrant visa, usually arranged through an employer.
