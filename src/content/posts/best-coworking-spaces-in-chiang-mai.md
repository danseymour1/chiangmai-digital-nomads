The established coworking spaces in Chiang Mai all have fast wifi, air conditioning and decent desks, so the real differences are atmosphere, price and location. For focused full-time work, Yellow in Nimman is the strongest choice; for a more social base, Punspace or Alt_ChiangMai in the Old City; on a tight budget, Hub53. Prices below are for a hot desk.

## 1. Yellow — best for focused full-time work

Yellow is a large, purpose-built space in Nimman with dozens of work areas, a garden and its own café, and the most professional feel in the city.

- **Price:** ฿429 for a day pass; ฿5,990 a month.
- **Area:** Nimman, within walking distance of the area's cafés and condos.
- **Who it suits:** full-time remote workers who want quiet, uninterrupted days.
- **Upsides:** big tables with plenty of sockets, an enforced quiet zone upstairs, many call booths, and 24-hour access with a free coffee each day on longer plans.
- **Downsides:** the most expensive option here, and quiet rather than social; day visitors are limited to 9am–6pm.

The call booths are standing-only and can get warm on long calls.

## 2. Punspace Wiang Kaew — best all-rounder in the Old City

Punspace is one of Chiang Mai's longest-running coworking brands. The Wiang Kaew branch occupies a renovated former soda bottle factory in the north of the Old City, and a second branch sits near Tha Phae Gate.

- **Price:** ฿289 for a day pass; around ฿3,899 a month.
- **Area:** Old City, close to temples and plenty of cheap places to eat.
- **Who it suits:** nomads who want a comfortable desk and some social life without paying Nimman prices.
- **Upsides:** comfortable desks, fast wifi, community events, and a large outdoor area with a coffee stand.
- **Downsides:** staffed hours are only 9am–6pm, with 24-hour access for members only.

Staff point members towards nearby food and drink spots, which makes lunch an easy way to meet people.

## 3. Alt_ChiangMai — best for community

Alt_ChiangMai is a coworking and coliving building behind Wat Phra Singh. Its sister site, Alt_PingRiver in Wat Ket, is newer and slightly more modern.

- **Price:** ฿320 for a day pass; ฿4,000 a month.
- **Area:** Old City, near the western side of the moat.
- **Who it suits:** newcomers who want to build a social circle quickly, and anyone staying in the coliving rooms.
- **Upsides:** good desks and chairs, an upstairs quiet area, an active group chat, and regular events and meet-ups.
- **Downsides:** coffee is not included in the pass, and the Old City location means a ride to Nimman.

The attached coffee shop is good enough to be a reason to work here in its own right.

## 4. Hub53 — best budget base

Hub53 combines coworking with coliving apartments on Chonprathan Road, on the canal side of the Nimman and university area.

- **Price:** ฿195 for a day pass; ฿1,999 a month for daytime access or ฿3,399 for 24 hours.
- **Area:** Suthep, a short ride from Nimman and close to Chiang Mai University.
- **Who it suits:** budget-conscious nomads who want a social space with a lively group chat.
- **Upsides:** cheap, 24-hour access on the right plan, a call room for members, and a kitchen with free coffee and drinking water.
- **Downsides:** the tables are small and closely packed, which can feel cramped on a busy day.

Several street food stalls are within a two-minute walk, so lunch and dinner are cheap and quick.

## 5. Smart&Start at Weave — best for a creative setting

Smart&Start is a coworking floor above Weave Artisan Society, a café, gallery and event space in a former ice factory on Wua Lai Road.

- **Price:** ฿250 for a day pass; ฿5,800 a month.
- **Area:** Wua Lai, just south of the Old City and home to the Saturday walking street market.
- **Who it suits:** creatives and anyone who wants events and good coffee on the doorstep.
- **Upsides:** a large, open and comfortable upstairs room with call booths, and regular community events downstairs.
- **Downsides:** not open 24 hours to visitors, and it is newer, so the coworking community is still forming.

There is a chocolate factory at the back of the Weave complex.

## 6. The Brick — best for startups and standing desks

The Brick is a startup space run by Chiang Mai University's Science and Technology Park (STeP), on a side street off Sirimangkalajarn Road.

- **Price:** ฿250 for a day pass; ฿2,500 a month.
- **Area:** the Nimman side of town, close to the university.
- **Who it suits:** founders and anyone interested in the local startup scene.
- **Upsides:** a large, bright room with plenty of natural light, adjustable standing desks, fast wifi, friendly reception and regular events.
- **Downsides:** not open 24 hours, and it feels more like a university incubator than a nomad hangout.

Its events are a good way to meet Thai entrepreneurs.

## 7. Life Space — best for occasional day sessions

Life Space on Nimman Soi 15 is a library-style café with desks facing the coffee counter. Visitors buy a five-hour or full-day pass rather than a membership.

- **Price:** about ฿180 for a day pass; no monthly plan.
- **Area:** central Nimman.
- **Who it suits:** those who mainly work from home but want an occasional change of scene.
- **Upsides:** good coffee, open until 10pm, and monitors and extension cords to borrow.
- **Downsides:** too few sockets, so finding a desk with power can take a couple of attempts.

One corner is filled with oddly shaped floor cushions, for anyone who works best lying down.

## 8. CAMP — best for a few hours on a budget

CAMP is a large, library-like workspace on the top floor of MAYA shopping centre, run as a café rather than a coworking space.

- **Price:** no pass; a minimum spend of around ฿50 on drinks or food gets a code for two hours of wifi.
- **Area:** MAYA at the Nimman and Huay Kaew Road junction, with parking in the mall car park.
- **Who it suits:** short sessions between errands.
- **Upsides:** cheap, central and open from 9am to 11pm.
- **Downsides:** mostly full of students, the food is overpriced for what it is, and there are no call booths.

The wifi code expires, so a long session means buying another drink.

## How to choose a coworking space in Chiang Mai

- **Deep focus and calls:** Yellow.
- **Social life alongside work:** Alt_ChiangMai or Punspace.
- **Lowest monthly cost:** Hub53 or The Brick.
- **An occasional change of scene:** Life Space or CAMP.

Location matters as much as the space itself, so it helps to choose one near the [best areas to live in Chiang Mai](/best-areas-to-live-in-chiang-mai/). A monthly desk is one of the smaller items in [the cost of living in Chiang Mai](/cost-of-living-chiang-mai-digital-nomads/), and those who prefer a livelier setting can mix in some of the city's many [laptop-friendly cafés](/best-cafes-work-chiang-mai/). Try a day pass before committing to a month.
